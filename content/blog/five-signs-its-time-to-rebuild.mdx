Every founder we've had on the show has hit the same fork in the road: keep patching what exists, or tear it down and start over.

Patching feels safer. It's also, often, the wrong call. Here are the signs that came up again and again in our conversations.

## 1. You're explaining the same problem in every retro

If last month's postmortem and this month's postmortem name the same root cause, you're not fixing the problem — you're re-treating the symptom.

## 2. The team already knows

By the time leadership calls a rebuild, the team has usually been saying it quietly for months. Ask them directly. Their answer is rarely a surprise.

## 3. Your fixes are getting smaller and more frequent

A shrinking fix cadence — patch, patch, patch, patch — is a sign you're chasing the system instead of correcting it.

## 4. The metric you're optimizing isn't the metric that matters

Several guests told us some version of the same story: the dashboard looked fine right up until the business didn't.

## 5. You'd make different decisions starting from scratch

This is the clearest test. If you wouldn't rebuild the current thing on purpose, that's information.

---

None of this means rebuilding is easy, or that it's always the right call. But if two or more of these are true, it's worth asking the question honestly instead of patching again.
